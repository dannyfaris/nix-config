# Ephemeral root — enforcing reprovision-from-source with btrfs archive-rollback and a declared persist whitelist

**Status:** Proposed — design note (`docs/design/`). **Enforcement live on metis (2026-07-30):** every boot archives `@root` and boots a fresh root; the wipe, auth chain, machine-id imposition, and first archive report are all hardware-verified (boot two; boot one's fail-safe bail found and fixed the device-ordering gap) (§De-risk). Soaking: purge cadence, ntfy `onFailure`, disk headroom. Next: the ADR extending ADR-034 after soak; Alcyone (#631) inherits at bootstrap. History: mechanism/runbook/probe mutation-validated 2026-07-26; the 2026-07-26 retrofit caused the 2026-07-30 auth-lockout (#553 comments), answered by relocating the auth path off file binds (§Design). [#553](https://github.com/dannyfaris/nix-config/issues/553) (owner) · [#633](https://github.com/dannyfaris/nix-config/issues/633) (audit probe, re-scoped by this note) · [#631](https://github.com/dannyfaris/nix-config/issues/631) (Alcyone, inherits the proven result at bootstrap) · extends [ADR-034](../decisions/ADR-034-no-host-data-backup.md); expect an ADR on acceptance. Mechanism selection ran the selecting-tooling process (2026-07-25); the evidence base is [`../research/ephemeral-root-prior-art.md`](../research/ephemeral-root-prior-art.md).

## Summary

ADR-034 stakes disaster recovery on two claims and adopts no backup tooling on their strength: **every host is reproducible from the flake**, and **durable data already has homes off the host**. Nothing enforces the first claim — today it is tested only by an actual disaster. This design makes it machine-enforced at every reboot: a systemd stage-1 initrd service renames the previous `@root` into a timestamped `roots-archive/` entry (retained N days, then purged — never hard-deleted at boot) and creates a fresh empty `@root`, so nothing on `/` survives a reboot except paths declared on an explicit persist whitelist — the nix-community impermanence module, backed by a `@persist` subvolume, with each declaration living in the module that owns the state. `/home` and `/nix` stay persistent and untouched: the second claim's territory, deliberately out of this design's reach. Adoption: metis first — a short report-only probe inventory of its live root seeds the whitelist, then enforcement flips with the archive as the safety net — and Alcyone inherits the proven mechanism and whitelist at bootstrap (#631).

## Motivation

State is the one domain this config does not govern. Users, packages, SSH access, and the stances themselves are all declared whitelists with mechanical enforcement (ADR-033); the root filesystem is the inversion — blanket-allow, implicit, mutable, unenforced. Any process can write anywhere on `/`, the write survives forever, and nothing declares or notices it. ADR-034 stakes disaster recovery on reprovision-from-source and deliberately adopts no backup tooling — a bet that is only sound if the flake really captures everything a host needs, which today is checked exactly once: during an actual disaster. #553 frames the fix; the 2026-07-25 operator discussion (recorded on #553) set the direction this note designs.

**Forces — what any design here must satisfy:**

- **Whitelist > blanket, enforced not asserted.** Undeclared state must not survive a reboot; the whitelist must be a declared, reviewable artifact, and the enforcement mechanical — the same grammar as every existing stance.
- **metis proves; new hosts adopt at bootstrap.** A fresh host must never boot mutable — bootstrap is the cheapest adoption moment it will ever have. But the mechanism and the whitelist are proven on metis first — the attended, console-break-glass host, per the fleet's established validate-before-inherit discipline: a short report-only inventory seeds the whitelist, retention bounds the retrofit risk, and Alcyone inherits a hardware-proven result instead of debugging an initrd service mid-bring-up.
- **Retention, not destruction** (operator constraint, first-class). The previous root is archived for N days, never hard-deleted at boot: the archive is simultaneously the audit trail (the exact delta of what the whitelist missed) and the recovery escape hatch. A recovery runbook must exist — the prior-art sweep found retention attested but **no documented recovery workflow anywhere in the fetched set**, so this is designed here, not copied.
- **`/home` stays persistent.** The originating erase-your-darlings baseline (research §5, the run's strongest vote); ephemeral `/home` is explicitly deferred — its worst losses are auth cliffs the field has no documented experience with.
- **Fleet reality as verified, not as remembered.** The pin's initrd is systemd stage-1 by default; roots are flat `@`-prefixed btrfs subvolumes via disko; nested subvolumes already exist under `@root` on the live host; no fleet host has a hibernation path. The mechanism must fit these facts (§De-risk evidence).
- **Module-owns-its-state.** A module that creates state declares its persist paths alongside its other config — no central list. The whitelist mechanism must therefore expose a *mergeable* option surface that modules contribute to independently. No prior art exists for this convention (research §6); it is ours to design.
- **Boot-critical fail-safe.** The wipe runs in the initrd, on the path to an unbootable host. Any error in the rollback must fail toward "boot the old root" — never toward blocking `sysroot.mount` — and a one-boot kill-switch must exist for recovery and debugging.
- **Proportionate scope.** Darwin is mechanically out (no btrfs, different boot stack — ADR-034 already notes this); nixos-vm and mercury are retiring (#634) and never adopt.

## Design

**Layout.** One new subvolume per adopting host: `@persist`, mounted at `/persist` with `neededForBoot = true` (an impermanence hard requirement, upstream-asserted since 2026-01). Archived roots live in `roots-archive/` at the btrfs top level (subvolid 5), which is not mounted in the running system — the purge timer and the recovery runbook mount it transiently. Alcyone's disko layout ships `@persist` from bootstrap; metis's retrofit creates it online (`btrfs subvolume create`, no repartition — verified possible against the existing flat layout).

**The fresh root.** Created empty at each boot with `btrfs subvolume create` — the canonical archive-and-recreate idiom ([recovery-workflows research](../research/ephemeral-root-recovery-workflows.md) §3). No blank snapshot exists: that idiom belongs to the rollback (no-archive) scheme family, and an empty subvolume is exactly what a blank snapshot would contain. NixOS activation plus the impermanence bind mounts repopulate the root from the closure and `/persist`.

**The rollback service** (systemd stage-1 initrd): ordered after the backing device unit — ordering-only `after`, never a `wants`/`requires` pull, which inverts the fail-safe into an initrd hang when the device is absent (VM-verified), belt-and-braces start timeout — and `Before=sysroot.mount`, it mounts the top level, renames `@root` → `roots-archive/<ISO-timestamp>` (collision-guarded; the name format is asserted), refreshes the archive's mtime — archive age counts from *archiving*, not the root's birth boot: `mv` preserves the top-dir mtime the purge selects on, so without the refresh a root with uptime past retention would be archived already-stale and reaped at the next purge (mutation-verified) — creates a fresh empty `@root`, unmounts, and exits. btrfs is forced into the initrd (`boot.initrd.kernelModules`) by the module itself: a pre-`sysroot.mount` service cannot rely on the root-fs machinery having loaded it, and without it the rollback silently bails every boot (VM-verified). Rename is atomic and O(1) — no data copy, negligible boot cost — and carries nested subvolumes (`/var/lib/machines`, `/var/lib/portables` exist today) along with the archived root intact. Two safety properties are non-negotiable: any failure leaves the existing `@root` in place and lets boot proceed (log + a degraded-marker file the probe reports), and a kernel-cmdline kill-switch (`ephemeral.skip-rollback`, checked first) skips the rollback for one boot — editable at the systemd-boot menu with no rebuild. The fail-safe is asserted *structurally* — the unit exits success and never enters the failed state, checked in the journal — not inferred from boot completion, which a `Wants`-pulled unit can fake while crashing (mutation-verified); and the healthy-boot case asserts the degraded marker's *absence*, so the signal cannot cry wolf. The kill-switch is recovery infrastructure, not merely a debug lever: a promoted root is consumed through exactly one kill-switch boot (§Rollout), since the service would otherwise archive it again immediately.

**The purge** runs in normal boot, not the initrd: a timer transiently mounts the top level and deletes archives older than N days, recursively (nested subvolumes require recursive delete — verified needed on the live host). The purge never touches the active root. Initial N = 30 days: the only figure attested in prior art (tsawyer87), and wide enough to cover monthly-cadence losses; revisited against probe evidence, not defended as more than a starting point. The retention window is sized together with `nix.gc`'s `--delete-older-than`: an archived root references store paths that garbage collection may strip, so a promotable archive needs its generation's closure alive ([recovery-workflows research](../research/ephemeral-root-recovery-workflows.md) §3 — a NixOS-specific coupling no surveyed ecosystem documents).

**The whitelist.** The impermanence module becomes a flake input; `environment.persistence."/persist"` holds the declarations. Module-owns-its-state is implemented by the NixOS module system itself: each repo module that creates state contributes its own `environment.persistence` fragment, and the options merge — no central list, and a module's state contract is readable next to the module. The seed baseline maps research §4 onto this fleet: `/etc/machine-id`, `/etc/ssh` host keys, NetworkManager (`system-connections` + `/var/lib/NetworkManager`), `/var/lib/bluetooth`, `/var/lib/nixos`, `/var/lib/systemd/{coredump,timers}`, `/var/log`, `/var/db/sudo`, `/var/lib/tailscale` — each landing in its owning module with its why-comment. The first metis inventory (2026-07-26) corrected the baseline both ways: **added** `/root` (real state, wrongly in the probe's early ignore set — a silent-loss class) and `/var/lib/private` (ntfy's DynamicUser state, declared 0700 in ntfy-server.nix — impermanence's 0755 parent default would hard-fail systemd's private-dir check); **dropped** `/var/lib/bluetooth` for metis (no pairings — declared by the owning module when a host has the capability). OS-core state with no owning repo module lives in `modules/nixos/persist-os-core.nix` as the stand-in owner. **One class rule, cut by the 2026-07-30 auth-lockout (#553 incident comments): nothing read before stage-2 mount units may depend on an impermanence *file* bind — early-boot-read state lives physically on a `neededForBoot` filesystem.** The two members of that class relocate accordingly: SSH host keys live at `/persist/etc/ssh/…` as sshd's *configured* key paths (`services.openssh.hostKeys` under `persist.enable`; sops-nix's age identity follows by its upstream derivation from `hostKeys` — deliberately one knob, since the incident's anatomy was two knobs drifting, and an eval assertion holds the invariant that sops key paths sit under a `neededForBoot` mount), and `/etc/machine-id` is imposed by a stage-1 initrd copy from `/persist` (PID 1 reads it before any mount can exist) with a self-extinguishing stage-2 seed leg for the fresh-host first boot (without it, an enforced host mints a new identity every boot — the initrd runs before the mint and after the wipe, forever). After this relocation zero *file* whitelist entries remain fleet-wide; file binds' empty-`touch`-then-bind behaviour on retrofit hosts is what manufactured the incident's impostor key. The empty-source interlock concern survives rescoped to *directory* retrofits (an unstocked directory bind is still a shadowing hazard). Declarations in shared modules are gated on the fleet-wide `persist.enable` option (`modules/nixos/persist.nix`): a non-empty declaration set makes impermanence assert a neededForBoot `/persist` mount, so ungated declarations would break every non-adopting host's eval — and the tempting mount-existence gate (`fileSystems ? "/persist"`) couples the declarations to `config.fileSystems` inside the module fixed-point that impermanence's own machinery evaluates against; an independent option derives from nothing. One declaration constraint, VM-verified: impermanence cannot persist a *file* at the filesystem root (a parent-directory handling error) — whitelist entries are directories, or file paths at least one level deep. `/var/lib/docker` is deliberately **not** an impermanence bind mount: on a rootful-Docker host it becomes a real `@docker` subvolume mounted at `/var/lib/docker` (bind-mount I/O overhead and snapshot-bloat are both attested; a large mutable tree wants a first-class mount). On metis the question is moot — Docker is rootless (ADR-021), its layer store lives under `/home` (persistent, out of this design's reach), and `/var/lib/docker` does not exist (verified 2026-07-26); the `@docker` treatment stays on the shelf for any future rootful host.

**The probe** (#633, re-cut by this note; design workshopped and endorsed 2026-07-26 after adversarial review): permanent drift-detection (a #551 rung-3 candidate, not pre-enablement scaffolding — #633's original one-shot audit gate died with the enforce-from-adoption direction), and the discovery mechanism for the desktop-state tail no prior art enumerates (research §4). One script, two targets, no mode option: a *daily live-root scan* (pre-loss warning — a path is reported while still alive, whitelistable before even one boot's state is lost; this is why the cadence is daily, to beat the next reboot) and a *per-boot newest-archive scan* gated on `ephemeralRoot.enable` (ground-truth confirmation of what the whitelist actually missed, suppressed/relabelled on a degraded boot). The prune set is interpolated into the script at *build time* from the module's own `config` view (`environment.persistence`, the mount layout) — never runtime `nix eval`, which would cost a full re-evaluation and describe git HEAD rather than the running generation; impermanence #240 is prior art for the diff idea, not the extraction mechanism. Noise is engineered down, not hoped away: the ignore-set skeleton is config-derived at build time (`environment.etc` names, tmpfiles rules, a runtime store-symlink test), glob-capable hand-tuned patterns cover the residue (random-suffixed paths would defeat literal-path deltas forever), and ntfy receives depth-limited rollups with the full listing in a local report file. Reports post to a second, *quiet* ntfy topic on the existing self-hosted instance (drift reports on the high-priority failure topic would train alert fatigue; zero server-side change — topics exist on first publish, the operator subscribes once), and only on *delta* against a persisted seen-set — the probe ships its own persist declaration for that state, module-owns-its-state applied to itself. Probe failures ride the existing `notify-failure@` path. The probe's first report-only run on metis (impermanence imported with empty declarations — `environment.persistence` must exist for the module to evaluate) is the retrofit inventory that seeds the whitelist.

**Rollout.** The VM test proves the mechanism first (§De-risk). metis then adopts: the probe runs report-only against its live root to inventory undeclared state and seed the whitelist; `@persist` is created online (no repartition); then enforcement flips, with the archive as the retrofit safety net. Named pre-flip step on metis: restock `/persist/etc/machine-id` with the **live** id (adjudicated 2026-07-30: the copies diverged in the incident, and the minimal-change answer won — canonicalising the running `2d20…` makes the flip identity-neutral (no revert, no DHCP re-key, the current journal thread continues), where restoring the original `ef39…` would have bought only archival contiguity the operating model never consults; the old journal drawer stays readable via `journalctl -D`). The auth chain itself was re-verified end-to-end on hardware after the key relocation (2026-07-30 cold boot: sops imported the `/persist` key pre-login, zero failed units). The flip and its first reboot happen with the operator physically at metis — that boot is the moment the unbootability class is live, and metis's break-glass is the physical console; enabling over SSH would volunteer for the worst version of the failure. Alcyone bootstraps last, inheriting the hardware-proven mechanism and whitelist in its disko layout from first boot (#631). The recovery runbook is [`docs/runbooks/ephemeral-root-recovery.md`](../runbooks/ephemeral-root-recovery.md), translated from adjacent ecosystems' mature workflows ([recovery-workflows research](../research/ephemeral-root-recovery-workflows.md)) rather than invented, and adversarially reviewed against the shipped module before landing (the review also surfaced the archive-mtime retention bug — §De-risk). Its frame: OS regressions roll back via NixOS generations (untouched by this design); state recovery is normally file-level reflink extraction from an archive under a single top-level mount (`cp --reflink=always` — zero-cost, CoW-shared); full promotion is the last resort — rename the current root aside, snapshot the archive into place (never a raw `mv`: the copy boots, the archive survives re-promotable), and consume it through one kill-switch boot. `btrfs subvolume set-default` is banned as a promotion mechanism — inert under the fleet's explicit `subvol=@root` mounts, with independent persistence and deletion traps (verified, recovery-workflows §2). Before promoting, `btrfs subvolume list -o` shows what flattens: snapshotting is non-recursive, so nested subvolumes in the archive return as empty directories (acceptable for `machines`/`portables` — systemd recreates them).

**How the forces are met.** Whitelist enforced mechanically at every boot; metis-proves-then-inherit sequencing with the retrofit bounded by retention; retention with a designed recovery path; `/home`/`/nix` never touched by the rollback; the mechanism is built for the verified fleet (systemd stage-1 service, rename-archival carrying nested subvolumes, no hibernation interaction to guard); module-owns-its-state rides the module system's option merging; fail-safe direction plus kill-switch guard the boot path; Darwin and retiring hosts are named out.

## De-risk evidence

Verified 2026-07-25 against the pinned inputs, the live host (metis), and source:

- **The pin's initrd is systemd stage-1 by default**: `boot.initrd.systemd.enable` evaluates `true` for metis with its only definition in the nixpkgs module itself (pin `e2587caef70c`) — nothing in-repo sets it. Consequence, load-bearing for the selection: the scripted-initrd hooks (`boot.initrd.postDeviceCommands` / `postResumeCommands`) that most published rollback implementations use still *exist* as options but are **inert** under systemd stage-1 — that entire prior-art family cannot be copied here.
- **Nested subvolumes exist under `@root` today**: `/var/lib/machines` and `/var/lib/portables` are subvolume roots (inode 256) on live metis — systemd creates them. Rename-archival carries them; the purge must delete recursively.
- **metis disko layout** (`hosts/metis/disko.nix`): flat `@root`/`@home`/`@nix`, no LUKS, no disk swap — zram only, "no hibernate" is a recorded decision. The hibernation-race corruption class from prior art (initrd touches the subvolume before the kernel checks the resume image) is structurally absent fleet-wide as provisioned; systemd stage-1 ordering additionally sequences resume correctly if that ever changes.
- **impermanence is not currently an input**; adopting it is a new single-maintainer flake dependency on the boot path (`neededForBoot`). Its whitelist mechanics, `neededForBoot` assertion (upstream, 2026-01), and maintenance state are in the research note §2.
- **31 GiB RAM on metis** — tmpfs-as-root would be feasible; it is rejected on the retention force, not on capacity.
- **The recovery-workflow translation pass ran** (2026-07-25, three targeted agents: snapper/openSUSE · ZFS boot environments · btrfs-native): [`../research/ephemeral-root-recovery-workflows.md`](../research/ephemeral-root-recovery-workflows.md). The runbook's three verbs translate cleanly; `set-default` and boot-menu `rootflags` editing are ruled out with mechanisms; the promotion×wipe interaction and the GC-window coupling surfaced there and are folded into §Design.
- **The one retention precedent, re-verified at source** (was UNVERIFIED in the research run's adjudication — research §3/§8): tsawyer87's guide confirms the mechanics verbatim — `mv` of the old root to a timestamped `old_roots/` entry, purge via `find -mtime +30` feeding a recursive subvolume-delete helper. Two findings carry forward: its rollback rides `boot.initrd.postResumeCommands` — the scripted-hook family that is inert on this fleet's systemd stage-1 — so even the single precedent cannot be copied, only re-idiomized; and it documents **no recovery workflow**, confirming the runbook has no precedent in the niche.

**The VM test — the de-risk gate before any host adopts.** A `nixosTest` flake output (`packages.x86_64-linux.ephemeral-root-vm`, run via `nix build .#ephemeral-root-vm`), built together with the rollback module as its first consumer:

- *The disk:* built from the same disko declaration the hosts use, via disko's image machinery (`system.build.diskoImages` — availability in the pinned disko revision is itself unverified; the fallback is the upstream nixpkgs pattern of formatting an attached empty disk in a setup phase). Either way the VM's layout is the fleet's layout, so test-vs-host drift is structurally impossible.
- *The definition:* the node imports exactly what a host would — the rollback module, impermanence, a fixture persist declaration. The test driver's direct kernel boot runs the full initrd, so the stage-1 rollback service executes on every VM boot with no bootloader emulation. Variant nodes carry the paths that cannot share a boot sequence — among them kill-switch (cmdline baked in), sabotage (a failure injected into the rollback service's archive or create step, e.g. a top-level mount forced to fail), retention-zero, and the promote-then-kill-switch sequence.
- *The assertions:* an undeclared file on `/` does not survive reboot; a persisted file does; the archive entry exists, timestamped, and **contains** the undeclared file (the safety net demonstrated, not assumed); a nested subvolume rides into the archive intact; the kill-switch node's file survives two boots; the sabotaged node still reaches `multi-user.target` on its old root with the degraded marker set — the fail-safe direction proven somewhere a failure costs nothing; a retention-zero node's aged archive is purged recursively; the promote-then-kill-switch sequence boots the promoted state and resumes wiping on the following boot; and a nested subvolume rides a rename intact but flattens to an empty directory under promotion-by-snapshot (both halves of the adjudicated contradiction — [recovery-workflows research](../research/ephemeral-root-recovery-workflows.md) §4).
- *CI posture:* a locally-run gate on metis for now (KVM native, minutes per run; the driver's interactive mode is where initrd iteration happens), exposed as a flake *package*, not a check — `nix flake check` builds checks wholesale in CI, so a checks entry would boot the six-node suite on every hosted-runner PR, stacked on an x86_64 leg that already runs ~30 minutes on main without it (the one hosted-runner build attempt was cancelled unfinished at 39 minutes, so its marginal cost is unmeasured — the restructure rests on the design's locally-run posture, not on a timing). CI still *evaluates* the package, so a pin bump that breaks its eval fails fast; the build graduates into `checks` when #546 self-hosts the x86_64-linux leg on metis — same machine, same KVM, warm store — rather than by solving hosted-runner virtualisation now. The test is x86_64-only as sketched; the mechanism is arch-independent, but the claim stays scoped if Taygeta (#547) later adopts.

**VM-verified (2026-07-26 — the gate ran).** The mechanism and test were built together and the suite was mutation-tested: the eight design assertions plus six mutation-driven additions, across six nodes; sixteen mutants (eleven catalogued, five hunted), **all killed** — four survived initially and were killed by added assertions (archive-name format, structural unit success, purge-timer scheduled-ness, degraded-marker absence-on-success). The recovery runbook's adversarial review (2026-07-26) then surfaced a latent retention bug the campaign had missed: `mv` preserves the renamed root's top-dir mtime — set at the root's *creation* boot and rarely advanced — so a root with uptime past `retentionDays` was archived already-stale and reaped at the next daily purge, a zero-day retention window for exactly the long-uptime archives that matter most (invisible to the original suite, whose roots live minutes). Fixed with an mtime refresh after the rename; two assertions added — a back-dated root's archive surviving a purge pass (kills the touch-removal mutant: the mutant's purge journal shows the fresh archive deleted), and the promote sequence re-verified in the runbook's exact displace-into-archive + touch shape. The probe slice (2026-07-26) added four assertions — undeclared-reported/declared-pruned, glob suppression with a non-matched control, delta discipline observed at the sink's POST bodies, and the archive half's shared-seen-set/marker behaviour — validated against three targeted mutants, each killed by its own assertion; the delta assertion's first version *survived* its mutant (a `sort -u` seen-set count is idempotent) and was strengthened to observe the posted bodies, the campaign working as designed. The suite now stands at six nodes, twenty assertions, twenty mutants killed. Design corrections from the run are folded into §Design above: ordering-only device dependency, btrfs forced into the initrd, no top-level-file whitelist entries. impermanence × systemd stage-1 orthogonality: confirmed (bind mounts are stage-2; the persisted-path assertion passed). Test-scope fallbacks, recorded: `/nix` rides the test driver's store share rather than a real `@nix` subvolume, and the test disk is attach-and-format rather than disko-image-built — so the disko image machinery remains unverified.

**Incident-verified (2026-07-30 — the retrofit auth-lockout, #553 comments; adversarially re-designed before rebuild).** The persist retrofit locked metis out: the file-bind choreography left an empty ssh host key on `@root` (impermanence `touch`es an empty underlying target before binding — verified at source), the next boot's early sops `neededForUsers` read resolved that underlying path *before any bind existed*, and sshd-keygen wrote an impostor key underneath — a failure generation rollback provably cannot fix, since every generation re-derives `/etc/shadow` from the same shadowed path. The same event minted a fresh `/etc/machine-id` (that boot ran as systemd first-boot — the moved-aside file class covered machine-id too). The fix design itself survived an adversarial review that caught its central mechanism *inert as first sketched*: `modules/nixos/sops.nix` explicitly pinned `age.sshKeyPaths` to the old `/etc/ssh` path, beating the derivation the sketch relied on — an ambiguous-verification lesson (the explicit value and derived default coincided, so evaluating the *resolved* value confirmed both hypotheses; provenance of a value, not its equality, is what verification must establish). Also review-established: option defaults never merge with `mkIf` definitions (the feared four-key hostKeys state is refuted at source), and metis's machine-id copies diverge (`/persist` holds the original `ef39…`, live runs the incident-minted `2d20…`) — the pre-flip restock is a named §Rollout step, not an assumption.

**Inventory-verified (2026-07-26 — the probe's first metis report, the retrofit inventory).** 6,198 raw lines, 6,034 of them `/var/cache` — the config-derived skeleton left residue-triage, not archaeology, and the residue confirmed the predicted baseline nearly entry-for-entry. Corrections folded back: `/root` and `/var/lib/private` joined the whitelist, `/var/cache`, `/bin`, `/usr`, etc-parent chains, and mount-point stubs joined the ignore skeleton, and `/var/db/sudo` exposed a known skeleton blindness (tmpfiles-created dirs are ignored even when their contents are state — the baseline list adjudicates over the report, which is a discovery aid, not the whitelist's source of truth). One coupling flagged for pre-flip adjudication: metis's `nix.gc --delete-older-than` and `retentionDays` are both 30 days, but §Cost requires retention *shorter* than GC for promoted archives to stay bootable — an archived root's generation predates the archive by that generation's age at reboot, so equality leaves a promotion-edge gap (file-level extraction is unaffected; store paths aren't file data).

**Enforcement-verified (2026-07-30 — the two enforcing boots on metis, console-attended).** Boot one fired the fail-safe for real: the rollback's ordering-only `after` against the cfg.device unit was *vacuous* — the root mounts by-partlabel, nothing queued the by-label unit, and the service ran before udev settled any symlinks — so it declined the wipe, wrote the degraded marker, and the machine booted its old root cleanly (a one-line diagnosis instead of a dead machine; the sabotage-node and structural-success mutants had rehearsed exactly this posture). The gap is the one class the VM cannot reproduce — real-hardware device timing — and the fix (#664) was verified against the systemd contract at source: `initrd-root-device.target` joins `after` (always in the stage-1 transaction; udev's persistent-storage rules mint by-partlabel *and* by-label in the same device event), and cfg.device aligns with the root mount's path family, whose unit is provably queued — either alone suffices, both ship. Boot two collected the campaign: `@root` archived (`roots-archive/20260730T100605Z`), fresh root, machine-id imposed by the initrd leg (its first real firing — identity continuous), sops decrypted from `/persist` pre-login, zero failed units, and the probe's first archive report came back **55 lines, every one a pre-adjudicated deliberate skip** — the day-one whitelist held, better than §Drawbacks braced for (the desktop tail lives on persistent `/home`). One live decision surfaced by the report: `/var/lib/systemd/linger/dbf` died as adjudicated — `users.users.dbf.linger = true` if lingering turns out to be relied on, nothing otherwise.

Still unverified or untested by scope (soak items, watched from 2026-07-30):

- **Purge behaviour under disk pressure** (archive growth vs churn — CoW makes the rename free, but N days of cache churn is real space; watched by the probe on metis).
- **The purge's `onFailure` → ntfy path** — not exercised in the VM (would need the notifier plumbing in the test closure); declared untested, validated on metis.
- **The purge timer's calendar cadence** (`OnCalendar`/`Persistent`) — untestable in a VM run; only its scheduled-ness is asserted.
- **Boot-menu `rootflags` editing as an interactive boot-once-into-archive path** — ruled out as a documented mechanism (initrd-driven mounts; no verified NixOS case — recovery-workflows §2); if ever wanted, it is empirically tested per-host first, never documented on faith.

## Drawbacks

- **A bespoke service on the boot-critical path.** The worst failure class is an unbootable host — a class this repo has never shipped a mechanism into. Fail-safe direction, the kill-switch, and the VM test bound it; they do not eliminate it, and the service is ours to maintain against nixpkgs initrd evolution.
- **The failure-mode inversion stands.** Every other stance fails closed and loud; a missed persist entry fails destructive and silent. Retention converts destruction into a discovery deadline (N days), not into zero — a loss noticed on day N+1 is still a loss.
- **A new single-maintainer input on the boot path.** impermanence is active but one person deep with a large issue backlog; the used surface is small (bind-mount generation) and the fallback (hand-rolled bind mounts) is real but costs the mergeable declaration surface.
- **The whitelist will be wrong on day one for the desktop tail** — no prior art enumerates it (research §4). That is by design (the probe discovers it, retention makes discovery cheap), but the first losses are real UX events on the operator's daily driver — metis feels the discovery cost so Alcyone doesn't — and honesty requires saying so up front.
- **Two-regime fleet.** Darwin hosts stay mutable; the eventual ADR must scope its claim to NixOS hosts rather than promising "one state policy."

## Cost

- **Disk headroom for N days of archived roots.** Rename-archival is free at archive time; the standing cost is the churn delta (caches, logs) held for the window — the snapshot-bloat pattern from prior art. The probe watches it; the purge bounds it.
- **A standing re-verification when the pin moves** stage-1 or btrfs semantics — the same class of per-bump ritual the Noctalia migration accepted, smaller surface.
- **Every new stateful module owes a persist declaration.** The module-owns-its-state tax is recurring and deliberate — it is the mechanism by which the whitelist stays deliberate instead of accreted.
- **Three windows must stay mutually coherent** — archive retention, `nix.gc --delete-older-than`, and the probe's report cadence. Retention shorter than GC keeps promoted archives bootable; reports faster than retention keep discovery inside the recovery window. A small standing coordination burden that did not exist before.

## Rationale & alternatives

The mechanism selection (selecting-tooling, 2026-07-25) weighed five candidates against the forces:

- **tmpfs-as-root** — rejected on the retention force alone: RAM cannot archive; a power cut destroys even the current root's contents with no recovery tier. Also the largest behavioural distance from the existing disko layout.
- **Scripted-initrd btrfs rollback** (the mt-caret/Guekka/tbx.at family, and the bulk of published prior art) — **rejected on verification, not on merit**: its `postDeviceCommands`/`postResumeCommands` hooks are inert under the pin's systemd stage-1 default. Copying the most-published pattern would ship a no-op.
- **impermanence module alone** — not a candidate on inspection: it declares persistence but contains no wipe. Without a wipe the whitelist is aspirational — precisely #553's critique of the status quo.
- **Filesystem-native impermanence** (upstream #255's snapshot-reset + rsync-back prototype) — experimental, author-labelled rough, no maintainer commitment; watched, not adopted.
- **Fully hand-rolled** (own wipe *and* own bind-mount whitelist) — rejected: re-implements a maintained module and, decisively, loses the merged-`environment.persistence` option surface that module-owns-its-state rides on.

**Chosen: the split.** A small hand-rolled systemd stage-1 archive-rollback service — the one component nobody ships, and the one that carries the operator's retention constraint — plus the impermanence module for the declaration surface. This is the Guekka split from prior art, re-idiomized for systemd stage-1, with retention added. Each half does the one thing the other cannot.

**Impact of doing nothing:** ADR-034's premise stays aspirational, the set-≠-enforced gap (#303) persists in the largest remaining domain, and every future host accumulates undeclared state from day one — including Alcyone, whose bootstrap is the cheapest adoption moment the fleet will ever see.
