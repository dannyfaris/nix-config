# Noctalia theming delegation — the Linux desktop's look, owned at runtime

**Status:** Proposed — operator direction agreed 2026-08-13 (the #770 stage-one dialogue, recorded in Epic G #819); not built. **Living through Epic G:** this note is the epic's working design record — it is updated at each gate (G2/G3 spike evidence, G4 re-targets, per-tool dispositions) as evidence lands, and freezes only when the successor ADR records the accepted decision. Reverses [ADR-044](../decisions/ADR-044-linux-runtime-theme-menu.md) for the Linux desktop on acceptance, and amends [ADR-041](../decisions/ADR-041-terminal-authority-tui-theming.md)'s Linux scope (the terminal bus becomes the fallback authority for untemplated TUIs — template-first, per the operator's leverage policy). Darwin explicitly untouched — celaeno keeps its conductor and its ADR-041 arrangement; the successor ADR lands per Epic G's G1 (#820), gated by the G2 abort criterion (#821). Sibling records: [colour-conductor.md](./colour-conductor.md) (the design being reversed), [docs/research/noctalia-v5-native-theming.md](../research/noctalia-v5-native-theming.md) (the standing evidence, beta.4 = the current pin).

## Summary

Theming authority on the Linux desktop delegates to Noctalia's native v5 engine. The operator picks any theme — builtin, community, wallpaper-derived — in Noctalia's own UI, and the entire desktop follows instantly, including already-open terminals via one Nix-shipped repaint hook riding Noctalia's theme-applied event. Nix declares *how themes reach surfaces* — and only where Noctalia does not already handle a surface natively: the candidate residue is a whitelisted template set, pre-declared include mount-points in the home-manager-owned configs, and the hook, but the exact native-vs-declared boundary is unresearched and is settled by the G3 spike, not assumed here. Nix declares *nothing about which theme*: no `[theme]` keys in `config.toml`, no source, no mode, no boot default. Mechanism declared — minimally, as a residue of native handling — taste delegated. The ADR-044 conductor (`home/nixos/theme-menu.nix`, the `theme` CLI, the seed, the polarity reconcile) is deleted on Linux, not deprecated in place. Colour returns to the "mutable skin" side of the declared-core/mutable-skin posture ADR-036 established for the shell.

## Motivation

ADR-044's conductor works, is runtime-verified, and is the most probed subsystem in the repo — and it is a parallel implementation of a capability the shell now ships in a form the operator would rather simply use. #770 named the frictions: the switch's only surface is a terminal CLI while the shell owns the launcher and control centre; the shell's own theme pickers are inert-by-convention, guarded by operator discipline against a sidecar that shadows declared config with no mechanical protection — and the lived experience of that half-dead surface was clunky, annoying and counter-intuitive: only portions of the theme control panel actually worked, and clicking the wrong item produced partial renders and ugly results (operator evidence, 2026-08-13); and the conductor's declared catalogue forecloses the theme ecosystem Noctalia ships — 10 builtins with hand-written terminal blocks, community palettes, wallpaper-derived schemes.

The #770 stage-one dialogue (2026-08-13) settled the user-facing model on the operator's terms, and it points away from repairing the conductor: theming ties to Noctalia specifically; Noctalia's switching UI is the primary gesture; community and wallpaper-driven themes are core UX; themes are runtime-imperative and the declared family catalogue retires on Linux; macOS and NixOS are treated separately and may drift. The operator explicitly accepts that this reverses Nix-as-authority and may end Stylix's Linux-desktop role: delegating to Noctalia's centre of gravity "saves solving a whole suite of problems elsewhere and gives a nice, pre-built UX to simply use."

**Forces — what the delegated arrangement must satisfy:**

1. **Pre-built UX, simply used.** Theme switching happens in Noctalia's own surfaces (control centre, launcher); no bespoke menu, no CLI required for the primary gesture.
2. **The full ecosystem is reachable.** Builtin palettes, community palettes, wallpaper-derived schemes — pickable at runtime, no repo edit — and Noctalia's template ecosystem (builtin and community) leveraged as much as it earns: a surface with a passing template is themed in true colours, not the ANSI-16 approximation.
3. **Instant and complete.** A pick re-themes everything visible with no perceptible wait — shell chrome, borders, GTK apps, portal-following apps, and the terminal *including already-open windows*, whose TUIs and statuslines follow via each tool's accepted mechanism (template where one passes, the ADR-041 bus otherwise) — instant is the bar; the mechanism per tool is whatever meets it. Only the accepted GUI-chrome relaunch exception remains.
4. **One writer, structurally.** No theme key is declared anywhere on Linux, so declared-vs-runtime disagreement is impossible by construction — the sidecar is the sole writer and the legitimate state. No reconcile machinery, no must-not-touch conventions.
5. **The pick persists.** Reboot, `nh os switch`, shell restart — the sidecar carries the selection; nothing stomps it.
6. **Nix still declares mechanism — as a residue of native handling.** Wiring is declared only where Noctalia does not handle a surface natively: which templates may write, where they mount into home-manager-owned configs, and the repaint hook — deliberate, whitelisted, reviewable, never blanket.
7. **Platforms decoupled.** Nothing here touches celaeno; the shared `lib/` colour pipeline remains load-bearing for Darwin only.

## Design

**Authority.** Noctalia owns theme selection and rendering. Theme state lives solely in the `settings.toml` sidecar, written by GUI/IPC — the intended channel, not drift. The `hooks.started` polarity reconcile, the inert-picker convention, and the dconf-as-input arrangement all retire with the conductor.

**The declared baseline shrinks to a residue of native handling.** `config.toml` carries no `[theme]` keys, and declares wiring only where a surface is not natively handled — Noctalia's own chrome needs nothing, and the residue below is the *candidate* set, finalized on G3's evidence, not assumed. Candidates: the template whitelist (`theme.templates.enable_builtin_templates = true` with explicit `builtin_ids`, plus `enable_community_templates` with explicit `community_ids` — membership per-tool on G3's evidence, whitelist-form always, blanket never), the repaint hook, and the existing non-theme baseline (idle, posture). A fresh host renders Noctalia's own defaults; the first runtime pick just works and persists (colour-conductor force 2 consciously retired, #819).

**Template-first, bus-fallback (operator policy, 2026-08-13): leverage Noctalia's templates as much as possible.** Where a template — builtin (foot, niri, starship, helix, btop, …) or community (zellij, yazi, fastfetch, obsidian, libreoffice, …) — exists for a surface and meets the standards below, the template themes it, in the theme's true colours rather than the ANSI-16 projection whose known legibility failures are on record (#615). The terminal bus (ADR-041) remains the mechanism for surfaces without a passing template and the live-repaint backstop — this amends ADR-041's Linux scope: the terminal is the TUI authority only for untemplated tools. Any Nix plumbing in a template's way — HM-owned config exclusivity, ADR-041 ANSI-mode settings — is restructured or removed per tool once its template passes. Per-tool acceptance gates, tested in G3 before adoption: (1) no mutation of HM-owned files (mount-points, or a deliberate per-tool ownership release, decided on evidence); (2) a reload path that meets the instant bar — native signal, next-render re-read, or hook-assist — or the tool stays on the bus (instant is the bar; the mechanism per tool is whatever meets it); (3) the rendered result meets the operator's standards on-metal. Template testing itself runs under syscall-level isolation (private `/proc`) — the starship incident proved env isolation insufficient for upstream apply scripts (De-risk).

**Mount-points are pre-declared.** Home-manager declares the include lines the templates expect — foot's `[main]` include and niri's `include "noctalia.kdl"` — pointing at the noctalia-written theme files, so each `apply.sh` finds its include present and only ever writes its own theme file. Declared skeleton, Noctalia-written content; no template mutates an HM-owned config (G3, #822, proves this on-metal — the research note's contrary inference is source-read only and must be tested in both directions).

**The terminal repaint hook.** A Nix-shipped script bound to Noctalia's `colors_changed` hook — source-verified at both the pinned and latest tags to fire strictly after all template files are written. It receives no environment, so it reads the mode and the noctalia-written foot theme file itself, then OSC-blasts live foot ptys — the #609-proven per-pty loop (OSC 4×16 + 10/11, pgrep→ps discovery) re-pointed at Noctalia's output. It reports failure honestly (#303; the probe-V2 lesson). At beta.4 the hook fires on every config reload (any section); beta.8 dedups to genuine palette changes — the script must be idempotent and cheap regardless, and the dedup is one reason G4 bumps to beta.8 early. G2 (#821) remains the on-metal go/no-go and keeps the epic's abort criterion: an unworkable loop stops the epic, rather than shipping new-windows-only terminals.

**Polarity.** `b664d3c` (Noctalia writing `color-scheme` natively on every resolve) is in no released tag — at every released tag the gtk template's `apply.sh` (NixOS-aware, in our whitelist) is the sole polarity writer, and it covers the portal-following apps at cutover. The native write is a watch item for the next release (beta cadence: median ~5–6 days across the series, last gap 11 days); when it lands, the gtk-template path simply becomes redundant rather than wrong. `portal-color-scheme.nix`'s disposition lands with the cutover.

**Wallpaper.** Already Noctalia-owned; wallpaper-driven theming is `theme.source = wallpaper` picked at runtime. #648 (theme-coupled wallpapers) is inverted and subsumed — dispositioned in the G6 sweep.

**Firefox carries no weight (operator ruling, 2026-08-13).** No verification, no fallback engineering, no decision deference anywhere in this loop: its current Stylix target's fate falls out of the G6 audit as accepted collateral (#819's DoD item 1 amended in kind — Firefox is not on the epic's acceptance bar). A possible move to Zen browser — which has a Noctalia community template — is a follow-up outside this epic.

**Deletions on Linux (cutover, G5).** `home/nixos/theme-menu.nix` (conductor + `theme` CLI + seed), the polarity reconcile, the Linux entries in `lib/theme-families.nix` `defaults`, and the conductor's consumer re-points in `foot.nix`/`niri.nix` (replaced by the mount-point includes). Stylix's Linux-desktop colour role exits or is reduced to a tracked inert residue per the G6 audit; `lib/theme-tokens.nix`'s colour roles become Darwin-only in practice while its static groups (spacing, radius, geometry) remain cross-platform.

**What is explicitly unchanged.** The fontconfig conductor (fonts are a separate authority, #390), Noctalia's shell role (ADR-036 item 1), the Darwin twin end to end. ADR-041 is *amended*, not retired: the terminal-bus mechanism survives as the fallback authority for untemplated TUIs and remote/SSH sessions, and Darwin's ADR-041 arrangement is untouched.

## De-risk evidence

A dedicated research-and-adversarial-verification workflow ran 2026-08-13 (10 subagents: four research areas, each independently re-derived by a verifier; one research payload was caught as a placeholder by its verifier and re-run). Every finding below marked *source-verified* was reproduced by an independent agent from a fresh clone at the cited tag; *observed-live* findings are snapshots of mutable surfaces (the community API) and carry that caveat explicitly. The G2/G3 spike program ran the same day (14 further agents across two runs: harness build → safety+validity review → two fix rounds → final approve → isolated probes → independent verification); its results are folded in below, including one real safety incident it caused and disclosed.

**Held:**

- **The theme-applied hook exists, released, at both tags.** Noctalia ships an 18-key `[hooks]` schema; `colors_changed` fires strictly *after* all templates (builtin → community → user) finish writing — ordering source-guaranteed by the single template worker (callback fires only for the winning generation). Executed async via `/bin/sh -lc` with **no environment variables**: the repaint script reads state itself. At beta.4 it fires on *every* config reload of any section; beta.8 (commit `460d1d4fc`) dedups to actual palette changes. Source-verified at `v5.0.0-beta.4` and `v5.0.0-beta.8`. The G2 question shrinks from "does a mechanism exist" to on-metal exercise.
- **The foot output fits the proven loop with zero parsing gap.** Noctalia's foot template emits the full 16 ANSI slots + fg/bg in a form the existing `theme-menu.nix` OSC parser already handles; the loop itself is the #609-runtime-verified conductor mechanism, re-pointed, not rebuilt.
- **The mount-point pattern is source-viable.** foot's and niri's `apply.sh` skip *all* mutation when the include line is already present (guards quoted at both tags) — a read-only HM config with a pre-declared include takes no write at all. Both include forms are syntactically expressible in our current `foot.nix`/`niri.nix` wiring.
- **`b664d3c` (native gsettings polarity write) is in no released tag** — it missed beta.8 by ~2 hours. At every released tag the gtk template's `apply.sh` (NixOS-aware, in our whitelist) is the sole polarity writer. Sidecar semantics (`setThemeMode`/`setThemeColorScheme` auto-erase bypass) are byte-identical at beta.8 — moot under delegation, where the sidecar is the intended state.
- **The community catalogue is inventoried** (observed-live, 2026-08-13): 46 templates — all six operator-named ids exist (`zellij`, `yazi`, `fastfetch`, `obsidian`, `antigravity`, `libreoffice`); 93 palettes, 100% parse-conformant — complete dark *and* light terminal blocks in the per-palette detail JSONs (the catalogue list endpoint carries only the M3 role summary). Community `template.toml` is processed by the *same* engine and hook semantics as builtin, via `/bin/sh -lc`, with **no sandboxing** — only path-traversal and md5 download-integrity checks; the trust surface is exactly as the Drawbacks state it.
- **The catalogue drifts, live-demonstrated:** the served palette list lags its own git repo by 3 entries, and the `libreoffice` template is *currently broken as served* — catalogue generation silently drops its `META-INF/manifest.xml`, so its `apply.sh` aborts (`set -euo pipefail`) on every run. The file exists upstream; the defect is API-side flattening. Community adoption must treat the catalogue as a mutable snapshot, re-inventoried at adoption time.
- **Per-tool reload facts, source-verified at our pins:** helix 25.07.1 **has** `USR1`/`:config-reload` and re-reads the theme from disk (the note's earlier "no reload signal" premise was wrong — helix is the *cleanest* hook-assist candidate, themes-dir-by-name, no include needed); starship re-reads per prompt render; btop reloads on `SIGUSR2`; yazi v26.5.6 hot-reloads themes via its `app:theme` action, externally triggerable per instance (`ya emit-to <id> app:theme`) — though the community template does not invoke it, so yazi is a hook-assist candidate; zellij's config watcher does not follow symlinks (open upstream bug #3992, unmerged fix PR #4205) — our HM-symlinked `config.kdl` is exactly that shape; fastfetch is one-shot (trivially fresh). None of the six community templates' `apply.sh` sends any reload signal.
- **A failure mode the note did not anticipate, now named:** starship's and btop's *builtin* `apply.sh` use `sed -i` against the discovered config path with no read-only awareness — against an HM symlink this *succeeds* by materializing a plain file over the link, and the breakage surfaces at the **next** `nh os switch`, not at apply time. G3 must test the rebuild-collision path, not just the apply path.
- **G2 sandbox-verified (2026-08-13, #821):** in an isolated headless compositor + Noctalia instance at the pin, `colors_changed` drove the repaint hook end-to-end with a genuinely fresh transition read back via OSC4 query (Nord→Dracula, pre ≠ post, post == the rendered theme file), and the honest-failure paths were exercised (dead-pty query, missing theme file). A single IPC theme change fires the hook **2–3×** at beta.4 (observed across both runs, never once) — the hook must be idempotent, and G4's dedup bump is evidence-backed. The epic's abort criterion is not triggered.
- **Mount-point pattern confirmed on-metal for foot, both directions** (include present → zero mutation + correct render; include absent → silent symlink materialization, exactly as predicted); niri's positive direction confirmed (zero mutation), its negative-mode exact failure shape still unobserved.
- **The starship incident (2026-08-13), disclosed in full:** the real upstream starship `apply.sh`, run against sandbox fixtures, escaped env isolation via its system-wide `/proc/*/environ` scan, found the operator's real `STARSHIP_CONFIG`, and materialized the real `~/.config/starship.toml` (one injected line; remediated same-session — symlink restored, content verified byte-identical to declared state). Its gate-1 verdict is thereby settled by the strongest possible evidence: **the starship builtin template is disqualified as-shipped** (the constructive route is a user-template with explicit `output_path` — starship re-reads per prompt, no apply.sh needed); **btop shares the hazard class** (`sed -i` + unscoped `pkill`, demonstrated in-sandbox). Standing lesson: **env isolation is insufficient for upstream apply scripts — template testing requires syscall-level sandboxing (private `/proc`)**.
- **Community applies in-sandbox:** yazi's template wrote only its isolated theme file; fastfetch honestly failed its precondition with zero writes; zellij ships no apply.sh at all. The zellij symlink-repoint produced zero watcher activity (consistent with #3992, not yet proof); the yazi `app:theme` test was inconclusive (instance-id discovery failed — retest owed); gtk's template file-write confirmed while its gsettings/dconf path went unobserved this run.
- The v5 engine audit (`docs/research/noctalia-v5-native-theming.md`, beta.4 = the current pin), the #609 OSC lineage, and the 2026-08-13 scouting record grounding the #770 dialogue.

**Unverified, load-bearing, each with its named gate:**

1. G2's operator-witnessed residuals: real-session subjective instant-ness on real hardware (the sandbox is headless), and an actual GUI picker click (every sandbox drive was IPC — same code path per source, unconfirmed empirically). Reviewed procedure written, post-cutover-scoped — #821.
2. G3's remaining fringe: niri's negative-mode exact failure shape; gtk's gsettings/dconf polarity path; a conclusive zellij watcher test; the yazi `app:theme` re-probe with working instance-id discovery; and the operator-witnessed `nh os switch` rebuild-collision procedure — #822.
3. Each adopted template against the three acceptance gates, community ids re-inventoried at adoption time (the catalogue is unpinned) — G3.
4. Target-tag behaviour at whatever G4 actually bumps to; the native gsettings write is a **watch item for the next release** (cadence ~5–6 days historically; `main` is 40 commits ahead of beta.8), not a released fact — G4 (#823).

**The claim-that-eliminates-an-option check** (the niri-sourcing lesson, design-loop.md §De-risk evidence): the keep-the-conductor option was eliminated by the operator's taste ruling — the pre-built UX is preferred and the catalogue is not wanted — not by an empirical claim requiring verification. The empirical claims in #770's earlier conductor-leaning analysis (durability, OSC superiority) were verified and are not disputed; they were re-weighted by the operator, which is the ruling the design loop exists to elicit. Symmetrically, the one factual premise this note *did* assert against an option — "helix ships no reload signal", which pushed helix toward bus-retention — was checked by the workflow and found **false**; it is corrected above rather than silently dropped. Peer review then caught a second false per-tool fact — yazi's supposed lack of any reload mechanism (it ships `app:theme` hot-reload at v26.5.6) — after the de-risk workflow's own verifier had missed it; also corrected above. The layering is the point: each verification rung has now caught something the rung below it passed.

## Drawbacks

- **Lifecycle re-coupling.** Theming re-binds to Noctalia's lifecycle — the exact coupling ADR-044 was built to escape after DMS died (ADR-029) and v4→v5 forced a rewrite (#644). A third bet, made consciously on v5's momentum; if Noctalia is ever replaced, theming is a rebuild event again.
- **Authority surrendered.** A reprovision no longer restores the look, and no mechanism guarantees runtime state matches anything declared — accepted knowingly as "declared core, mutable skin" extended to colour.
- **Beta authority.** The engine is beta, with active config-migration churn and thin tests; the per-bump ritual grows (hook surface, template inventory, sidecar semantics, polarity writer).
- **A network edge opens, wider than palettes.** Community palettes *and templates* fetch from `api.noctalia.dev` into state — and a community template is remotely-fetched *write behaviour* (output paths, pre/post hooks, `apply.sh` run via `/bin/sh`), not just colour data. Accepted as a deliberate, operator-ruled exception to the whitelist stance (2026-08-13), bounded by explicit per-id enablement and per-tool G3 testing before adoption; stanced in the ADR (relates #579/#568). The catalogue is mutable and unpinned — observed 2026-08-13 already drifting from its own source repo (three palettes lagging; the libreoffice template broken as served) — so adopted community ids are snapshots to re-inventory, not pins.
- **Fidelity moves from curation to taste.** The #331 slot-correction regime ends on Linux; recourse for an off-looking theme is picking another, not patching slots in git.
- **Two theming systems fleet-wide.** Darwin keeps the conductor; Linux goes native — accepted drift, but a real divergence in the shared `lib/` files' load-bearing scope.

## Cost

The standing price once chosen: the repaint hook is ours to maintain against a beta hook surface, and every Noctalia bump carries the grown ritual. The G6 Stylix-exit audit is a real one-time sweep, and adopted community ids carry a small recurring re-inventory cost (the catalogue is a snapshot, not a pin).

## Rationale & alternatives

- **Keep the conductor; fix reachability and drift-protection.** The pre-dialogue analytical lean: register the `theme` CLI in the capability palette, mechanise the sidecar guard, add readback honesty. Rejected by the operator's ruling — it solves the frictions but delivers none of the wanted ecosystem (community palettes, wallpaper-driven schemes, the pre-built picker UX) and maintains a parallel implementation indefinitely.
- **Hybrid: the base16 catalogue as custom palettes, v5's engine as renderer** (the research note's landing zone). Rejected — it keeps the catalogue-curation pipeline alive precisely where the operator wants it retired, for the marginal gain of preserving five families four of which exist as builtins anyway.
- **Full delegation (chosen).** Satisfies all seven forces; the only force it strains is instant-terminal (force 3), which the repaint hook addresses and G2 gates with an abort criterion rather than a hope.
- **Do nothing.** The #770 frictions stand: CLI-only reach, convention-guarded pickers, no ecosystem access — and the sidecar-shadowing exposure remains anyway, guarded only by discipline.

**Impact of doing nothing:** the operator keeps maintaining a bespoke layer that delivers a worse daily UX than the shell it sits beside.

## Unresolved questions

- **The native-vs-declared wiring boundary:** which surfaces Noctalia handles with no repo wiring at all, and which need declared residue (templates, mount-points) — researched in G3, whitelist finalized on its evidence, never assumed.
- **Per-tool template disposition:** which TUIs move from the ADR-041 bus to a template and which stay — each against the three acceptance gates (no HM mutation, instant met, looks right), with the plumbing-removal shape (mount-point vs ownership release) decided per tool in G3. - **G4 (#823):** bump to v5.0.0-beta.8 (the `colors_changed` dedup), ritual + claim re-verification there; the native gsettings polarity write is a watch item for the next release, not a gate.
- **Stylix exit scope (G6):** full drop from Linux desktop hosts vs a named residue; the consumer sweep decides on evidence.
- **Setup wizard:** its off-declaration existed to prevent day-one theme-override persistence, now the intended channel — re-enabling is pure taste, carried as a non-decision to the cutover.
- **ADR timing:** successor drafted with this note, landed once G2's evidence is in hand (the abort criterion makes earlier freezing dishonest).

## Future possibilities

- **A polarity keybind** — a registered capability (#442) calling `noctalia msg theme-mode-toggle`, pure gesture sugar over the delegated authority.
- **Wallpaper-driven theming as the daily default** — nothing prevents living in `theme.source = wallpaper` permanently; no repo change needed.
- **Zen browser** — a Noctalia community template exists; a browser move would bring the browser into the delegated-theming fold as a follow-up outside this epic (Firefox itself carries no weight, per the ruling above).
- **Darwin re-convergence** — if v5's engine ever fits macOS, the same delegation could retire the Darwin conductor; nothing here forces or forecloses it.
