# Recovery workflows for archived btrfs roots — translated prior art (snapper, ZFS boot environments, btrfs-native)

Status: **research note, not a decision.** Captured 2026-07-25 from a targeted three-agent pass (snapper/openSUSE · ZFS boot environments · btrfs-native promotion), commissioned by [`../design/ephemeral-root.md`](../design/ephemeral-root.md) §De-risk to *translate* mature recovery workflows into the fleet's archived-root scheme rather than invent one — the broad sweep ([ephemeral-root-prior-art.md](./ephemeral-root-prior-art.md) §3) found no recovery runbook in the NixOS-impermanence niche. Every load-bearing claim carries a source; the two contradictions between agents are adjudicated in §4 with the evidence stated. Feeds #553.

## 1. The runbook shape that survives translation

Three verbs, all filesystem-native, none requiring snapper/ZFS machinery:

- **Browse** — mount the btrfs top level read-only (`mount -o subvolid=5,ro`), inspect `roots-archive/<timestamp>/…`. The direct analog of `bectl mount` / snapper's `.snapshots/<N>/snapshot/` browsing ([bectl(8)](https://man.freebsd.org/cgi/man.cgi?query=bectl), [openSUSE Leap ref](https://doc.opensuse.org/documentation/leap/reference/html/book-reference/cha-snapper.html)).
- **Extract** — `cp --reflink=always` from archive to live root **within a single top-level mount**: zero-cost (CoW-shared), and the single-mount form sidesteps the pre-5.18 cross-mount EXDEV limitation entirely ([btrfs Reflink](https://btrfs.readthedocs.io/en/latest/Reflink.html), [LWN](https://lwn.net/Articles/502746/)). This is `snapper undochange` minus the snapshot database — with snapper's own warning carried: review before restoring system files; some (the `/etc/mtab` class) must never be restored ([openSUSE Leap ref](https://doc.opensuse.org/documentation/leap/reference/html/book-reference/cha-snapper.html)).
- **Promote** — rename the current root aside, then **snapshot the archive into the canonical name** (`mv root root.broken; btrfs subvolume snapshot roots-archive/<ts> root`): the LMDE production runbook's move-then-snapshot pattern ([lmde-system-recovery](https://github.com/pavinjosdev/lmde-system-recovery/blob/main/README.md), [Fedora/Chris Murphy](https://discussion.fedoraproject.org/t/rollback-btrfs-root-snapshot/72074)). Snapshot-into-place, never a raw `mv` of the archive — the copy boots, the archive survives re-promotable within the retention window. A snapshot of a writable source is writable, i.e. bootable, directly.

The mainstream BE recovery pattern — *boot the good environment, then destroy the bad one* — maps to: promote, and let retention purge the displaced root ([FreeBSD BE runbook](https://www.freebsdsoftware.org/blog/freebsd-boot-environments/), [Solaris beadm lab](https://pavelanni.github.io/oracle_solaris_11_labs/be/be/)).

## 2. What does NOT transfer (each verified, with mechanism)

- **`btrfs subvolume set-default` as the promotion primitive** — snapper rollback's core move, **inert on this fleet**: an explicit `subvol=`/`subvolid=` mount option always wins over the default-subvolume setting, and disko pins `subvol=@root` ([btrfs-subvolume(8)](https://btrfs.readthedocs.io/en/latest/btrfs-subvolume.html), [Launchpad #1734496](https://bugs.launchpad.net/bugs/1734496) — the documented reason snapper rollback fails on Ubuntu; Oracle's rollback doc strips `rootflags` via grubby to make set-default win, [Oracle](https://docs.oracle.com/en/operating-systems/oracle-linux/9/btrfs/btrfs-MountingAlternateSnapshotsastherootFileSystem.html)). Three further traps even where it works: it may not persist until a mount/unmount flush ([Arch BBS 239755](https://bbs.archlinux.org/viewtopic.php?id=239755)); it binds subvolid and silently follows a rename into the archive; a subvolume that is the default/current root cannot be safely deleted. Verdict: promotion is by **name**, never by set-default.
- **GRUB's boot-into-read-only-snapshot verify step** — grub-btrfs generates per-snapshot menu entries by scanning `.snapshots`; no systemd-boot equivalent exists ([grub-btrfs](https://github.com/Antynea/grub-btrfs)). The verify step becomes offline browse + inspect.
- **Editing `rootflags=subvol=` at the systemd-boot menu as boot-once-into-archive** — unreliable where an initrd mounts root from generated units rather than the raw cmdline (this fleet's systemd stage-1 does exactly that); no forum-verified NixOS case found; systemd-boot users in the wild avoid it or switch to GRUB ([Arch BBS 245366](https://bbs.archlinux.org/viewtopic.php?id=245366), [Fedora 92926](https://discussion.fedoraproject.org/t/systemd-boot-btrfs-root-subvolume/92926)). `bootctl set-oneshot` is real and native but selects a *loader entry* (kernel+initrd), not a data subvolume ([bootctl(1)](https://www.freedesktop.org/software/systemd/man/251/bootctl.html)) — a true boot-once-into-archive would need the initrd rollback service itself to consume a marker, and temporary-activation revert paths are historically buggy even in mature BE tools ([illumos #13367](https://www.illumos.org/issues/13367)). Verdict: not a primary recovery path; empirical-test-first if ever wanted.
- **`zfs promote` / clone-dependency inversion** — no analog needed: renamed btrfs subvolumes carry no parent-clone dependency; each archive is already independent ([zfs-promote(8)](https://openzfs.github.io/openzfs-docs/man/master/8/zfs-promote.8.html)).

## 3. Fleet-specific findings the runbook must encode

- **Promotion under a per-boot wipe is self-defeating without the kill-switch.** The rollback service archives whatever `@root` it finds at every boot — including a just-promoted root. Full promotion therefore pairs with a kill-switch boot (skip the rollback once, consume the promoted state); the boot after that resumes normal wiping. The kill-switch is load-bearing recovery infrastructure, not merely a debug lever. (Synthesis finding — follows from the design's own mechanism plus the promotion pattern above.)
- **Generation rollback substitutes for promotion in the common case.** `/nix` is a separate untouched subvolume: a bad config/package change is rolled back by booting an older NixOS generation — no root promotion involved. An archived root holds *mutable state only*; promotion is the narrow lever for state not yet in `/persist`. The expected dominant recovery is file-level extraction, not promotion. ([tsawyer87](https://tsawyer87.github.io/posts/btrfs_impermanence/), [mt-caret](https://mt-caret.github.io/blog/posts/2020-06-29-optin-state.html) for the two scheme families.)
- **Nix GC can strip a promoted root's closure.** An archived root references store paths; GC honours only live roots, so a weeks-old archive may point at a partially-collected closure. Check the target generation still exists before promoting. Retention window and `nix.gc --delete-older-than` window should be sized together.
- **The canonical btrfs archive scheme creates the fresh root empty** (`btrfs subvolume create`), not from a blank snapshot — the blank-snapshot idiom belongs to the *rollback* (no-archive) scheme family inherited from ZFS. Under archive-and-recreate, `@root-blank` is unnecessary machinery ([tsawyer87](https://tsawyer87.github.io/posts/btrfs_impermanence/) — verbatim script).

## 4. Contradictions adjudicated

- **"Renaming a root strands nested subvolumes" (snapper agent) vs "rename carries them" (ZFS agent).** Adjudication: rename carries them. The cited failure ([jrabinow/snapper-rollback #27](https://github.com/jrabinow/snapper-rollback/issues/27)) is a tool's stale-path bookkeeping bug, not a btrfs semantic; the canonical archive script's recursive purge exists precisely because archives *do* contain their nested subvolumes. The **real** nested-subvolume hazard is in *snapshots*: snapshotting is non-recursive — a nested subvolume becomes an empty directory in the copy ([btrfs Subvolumes](https://btrfs.readthedocs.io/en/latest/Subvolumes.html), [Fedora 72074](https://discussion.fedoraproject.org/t/rollback-btrfs-root-snapshot/72074)). Consequence: archival-by-rename is safe with nested subvolumes; **promotion-by-snapshot flattens them** — on this fleet that means `/var/lib/machines`/`portables` come back as empty dirs (systemd recreates them; acceptable), and the runbook checks `btrfs subvolume list -o <archive>` before promoting so the operator knows what flattens. The VM test asserts both halves.
- **snapper's offline rollback form.** The SUSE prose documents only boot-into-snapshot-then-rollback; the man page grammar accepts `snapper rollback <N>` directly ([snapper.8](https://man.archlinux.org/man/snapper.8)). Minor; recorded because the prose-vs-manpage gap is the kind of thing that misleads translation.
